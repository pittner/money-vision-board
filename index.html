12 honest money questions before you choose your goals

Answer them alone, with a notebook, in 15 minutes. Be honest, nobody else reads it. The answers that surprise you point to the goals that will actually stick.

  1. How much did I really earn and spend in 2026, roughly?
  2. What did I buy this year that I am glad I did, and what do I regret?
  3. When do I spend money I did not plan to spend, and what is happening then?
  4. What is one money worry that keeps coming back at night?
  5. Who taught me how to think about money, and what did they get wrong?
  6. What would three months of savings change in how I feel?
  7. Which debt bothers me most, and why that one?
  8. What could I earn more for, if I asked or offered it?
  9. What do I want money for, really: safety, freedom, time, experiences?
  10. What is one small money habit I could keep even in a bad month?
  11. Who do I talk to about money, and who should I talk to more?
  12. If 2027 goes really well, what is different about my money by next December?

Plan your money year in 45 minutes

  1. 10 minutes: look back honestly. Open last month's bank statement and write the five biggest categories. The numbers point to your plan.
  2. 15 minutes: answer the questions. Go through the 12 questions below. Short, honest answers are enough; nobody else reads them.
  3. 10 minutes: pick three goals. Not ten. One for saving or debt, one for spending, one for earning more or investing.
  4. 10 minutes: make it visible. Turn your most important goal and its first step into a phone lock screen, so you see it every time you open a shopping app.

Questions

What should go on a money vision board?

Put the goal in your own words with a number and a date, a first step you can do this week, and your reason why. This page has 36 money goals in 6 groups (saving, debt, budget, earning more, investing, mindset), each with a first step, so you can pick three and make them visible.

Does a financial vision board actually work?

A board does not save money by itself, but seeing your goal every day keeps it in front of you when you decide to spend. The habit that works best is putting it on your phone lock screen, which you look at right before most purchases.

How many money goals should I set?

Three is enough: one for saving or paying off debt, one for spending less, and one for earning more or investing. Fewer goals get more attention and stick longer.

What are realistic financial goals for 2027?

Small and specific beats big and vague: save 10% of every paycheck, build a 3-month emergency fund, pay off the credit card, cancel three forgotten subscriptions, ask for a raise. Write a number and a month next to each.
